Is your system used for one of the sensitive purposes on the AI Act's high-risk list?

The AI Act singles out certain uses, across eight areas, as high-risk because they can seriously affect people's lives. Only the specific uses listed under each area count, not the whole area. Tick anything that matches what your system is actually meant to do.

In the words of the law
AI systems referred to in Annex III shall be considered high-risk. Annex III lists eight areas and, under each, an exhaustive list of specific use cases. Art. 6(2); Annex III
Examples / how to decide
Likely yes: hiring or staff-management tools, credit scoring for people, exam monitoring. Likely no: a system that only assesses companies, not people.

High-risk: a sensitive, listed use

Your system is used for one of the sensitive purposes on the high-risk list and doesn't qualify for an exemption. These duties are due to start on 2 December 2027.

Draft guidance: is my system high-risk?
For the provider
Manage risks, use good-quality data, keep documentation and logs, keep a human in the loop, make it accurate and secure.
Register it in the EU database and CE-mark it before launch.
For the deployer
Use it as instructed, oversee it, and run a fundamental-rights impact check where required.
Be transparent with people

One or more transparency situations apply. These stack on top of your main category. They start on 2 August 2026.

Tell people they're dealing with an AI, and mark AI-made content so machines can detect it.
Worth double-checking with an expert: you answered "I'm not sure" on profiling. If your system profiles people, it is automatically high-risk and the exemptions don't apply.
Open-source note: a free/open-source licence doesn't remove high-risk, banned or transparency duties. It only lightens some documentation duties for general-purpose models.
